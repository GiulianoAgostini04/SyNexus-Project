# SyNexus-Project
---
Presented at the Innovating Biomedical Devices Competition and Showcase 2026. A Non-invasive and Adaptive Brain-Computer Interface acquiring signals from the primary motor cortex to address neuropathologies and neurotrauma. Covers hardware/software architecture, the experimental pipeline, and future developments in componentry and connectivity.

Title: Non-invasive and Adaptive Brain-Computer Interface Based on Electroencephalographic Signals from the Primary Motor Cortex

Context:
Project designed and created within the Innovation Biomedical Devices Student Competition and Showcase 2026

Description:
The project addresses the clinical challenge of restoring communication for patients affected by neuropathology and neurotrauma such as Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis, Locked-in Syndrome, and spinal cord injury, conditions that disrupt the pathway between motor intention and execution, while cortical activity in the Primary Motor Cortex (M1) often remains accessible. SyNexus targets M1 signals, acquired via EEG, to bypass this interrupted pathway and translate motor imagery into digital commands. On the hardware side, dry Ag/AgCl electrodes are integrated (Cz, C1/C2, C3/C4, FC3/FC4) in an elastic structure in hypoallergenic polyester, in order to replace the conventional EEG cap. The signal is transmitted via twisted snap cables to an OpenBCI Ganglion board. The software environment integrates the OpenBCI GUI for real-time visualisation, MATLAB (EEGLAB/ERPLAB) for ERP analysis and training, and a Python/Brainflow application for cursor control, structured around four pillars: Recording, Analysing, Training, and Controlling. The methodology follows a full operational pipeline, from patient intake to training and real-time control, with cursor movement and object selection achieved through a distinguishable EEG event: the Event-Related Potentials (ERP). Future development points toward biopolymer, patch-based electrodes, wireless communication, and a cross-platform, AI-assisted application, extending SyNexus toward a renewed tool.
